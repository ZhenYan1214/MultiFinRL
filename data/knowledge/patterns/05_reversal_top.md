# Top Reversal

## Pattern
A top reversal is an advance that turns into a sustained decline inside the window. Price rises for several sessions, peaks, and then falls for several sessions, giving back at least about half of the rise, so the latest close sits in the lower half of the range. Common shapes are an inverted V, a rounded top, a double top (an "M" with two similar highs separated by a pullback), and a head and shoulders top (a peak, a higher peak, then a lower peak above a neckline). A small pullback near the high is not a top reversal.

## Volume
Volume often fades on the final push to the high or on the second peak of a double top, showing weaker buying. Volume usually expands as price falls through the neckline or the middle of the range.

## Price-volume reading
A decline from the peak on expanding volume confirms that sellers have taken control and the advance is over. A decline on light volume is weaker and may only be a pullback inside the uptrend. A lower second peak on lighter volume is an early warning of the top.
