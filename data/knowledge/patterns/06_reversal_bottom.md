# Bottom Reversal

## Pattern
A bottom reversal is a decline that turns into a sustained advance inside the window. Price falls for several sessions, bottoms, and then rises for several sessions, recovering at least about half of the fall, so the latest close sits in the upper half of the range. Common shapes are a V, a rounded bottom, a double bottom (a "W" with two similar lows separated by a rebound), and a head and shoulders bottom (a trough, a deeper trough, then a higher trough below a neckline). A small bounce near the low is not a bottom reversal.

## Volume
Volume is often heavy near the low as selling climaxes, then lighter on the second low of a double bottom. Volume usually expands as price rises through the neckline or the middle of the range.

## Price-volume reading
A rise from the low on expanding volume confirms that buyers have taken control and selling pressure is exhausted. A rise on light volume is weaker and may only be a bounce inside the downtrend. A higher second low on lighter volume is an early sign of the bottom.
